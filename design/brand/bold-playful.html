Couch Coach · brand route

Bold and playful

Three foam shapes, three loud colours, and copy that sounds like a grown-up who is fun on the floor. Loud enough that a four-year-old shouts "FIRE TRUCK", tidy enough that a parent believes the instructions.

1Logo

The idea: a coach's whistle built from the three foam pieces. Slab for the mouthpiece, hoop for the body, triangle pillow on top. The wordmark stacks COUCH over COACH so the one letter that changes lines up, blue U over red A.

On light. Paper or white behind it; ink letters.

On dark. The mark keeps its colours; the two accent letters lighten so they stay readable.

Small sizes. The mark alone is the favicon; no outline, no detail to lose.

The wordmark here is live text in the fallback face. For launch it gets drawn once as outlines so it looks the same on every phone.

2Colour

Four crayon-box colours plus ink and warm paper. Everything gets an ink outline and a hard ink shadow, so the page looks like stickers on a table.

Each theme has a shape as well as a colour, so a card never depends on colour alone.

Default colours in the drawings

Play couch: Fort Blue #1F55D6. Round add-on: Sunshine #FFC61A. A second couch starts the same blue until the parent picks its own colour. Parents can still change all of them; these are only the starting point.

Ratios are worked out in this page from the hex values (WCAG 2 formula). Body text needs 4.5:1.

3Type

Display: Fredoka Bold (SIL Open Font License), one self-hosted file later. Shown here in the fallback actually used: Arial Rounded MT Bold on Apple devices, then the system rounded or system UI face. Text: the phone's own system font, so there is nothing to download.

Rules

Display face for anything a kid might shout. System face for anything a parent has to read. Sentence case everywhere except the logo.

4Voice and copy

We talk like the grown-up who gets down on the floor: short, loud where it is fun, and straight about what we have and have not tried. Jokes go in headlines and never in instructions or safety notes.

6What this route is not

Not quiet. No beige, no pastels, no thin lines. If it would suit a linen catalogue, it is the other route.

Not a cartoon. No mascot, no faces on the furniture, no wobbly hand-drawn letters. The foam shapes are the characters.

Not jokey about the facts. Piece counts, steps and "Not yet kid-tested" are said plainly, every time.
